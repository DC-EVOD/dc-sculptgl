What *is* extractable:

  the 109 MB packs gave pure compression noise

**Unconfirmed, worth his minute:** three files named `free.ZBP` / `free (1).ZBP`
are all exactly 109,336 KB with byte-identical first-256KB string runs — very

that they are different packs. A SHA256 comparison settles it.

### STL — the format costs you three of four signals
No UVs, no materials, no object/node names. Decomposition reads boundaries in
priority order: node hierarchy → material groups → connected components → UV
islands. **STL kills three; only connected components survive.** And print-prep
STLs are often unioned into one watertight solid, which destroys even that.
Measure connected components per STL before treating STL as a viable donor.

**For the ZBrush macro**, when it comes: duplicate the subtool (never destroy the
original) → rename `_LOW` → polygroup by normal angle → ZRemesher with
KeepGroups/Detect Edges so plate breaks survive → original stays as the projection
source. **Do not hand-write ZScript interface paths from memory** — they are
version-specific. Have him record the macro by hand once; the recorded `.txt`
carries his install's real paths, and you parameterise that.

**On topology**, per the `game-art-topology` law: the silhouette is king, judged in
flat mode. ZRemesher does not produce game-ready topology — it smears across plate
breaks unless polygrouped first, and it generates poles that pinch. For hard-surface
plates the real method is **boolean + bevel + weighted normals in Blender**, then
bake. Tri budgets come from his archive's `typical_triangles` and his own measured
62:1 Detail_Test ratio, not from a number you pick.
