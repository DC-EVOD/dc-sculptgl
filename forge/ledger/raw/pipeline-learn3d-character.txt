### Concept development

Create strong 2D references for the entire character pipeline. In this module, students learn how to generate the core idea, refine it into a clean production-ready concept, split the character into parts, and prepare additional views for accurate 3D generation. This stage saves time on every step that follows and gives a clear visual foundation for the full workflow.

You walk away with:

A complete set of clean 2D references ready for 3D generation.

Tools:

### 3D model generation

Turn your 2D references into initial 3D meshes using AI generation tools. Students learn how to choose the right generator, evaluate results, regenerate weak outputs, and bring selected meshes into Blender for further work. The goal is to get a usable base model and separate generated parts that are ready for cleanup and sculpting.

You walk away with:

A raw 3D base model and generated character parts ready for refinement.

Tools:

### Sculpting

Clean up the generated geometry and reshape the character into a stronger final form. Students correct major artifacts, improve silhouette and anatomy, align the model into a proper pose, and prepare separate parts for assembly. This is where the AI result starts becoming an intentional character instead of just a generated mesh.

You walk away with:

A cleaned and sculpted high-poly character with improved anatomy and readable silhouette.

Tools:

### Retopology & optimization

Convert the sculpted high-poly result into a cleaner, lighter low-poly mesh suitable for games and animation. Students learn how to use AI-assisted retopology, optimize mesh density, align low and high versions, and manually fix important topology issues. Special attention is given to silhouette preservation, deformation areas, and production-friendly geometry.

You walk away with:

An optimized low-poly mesh that matches the sculpt and is ready for UVs, baking, texturing, and rigging.

Tools:

### UV unwrapping

Prepare the low-poly model for texturing by creating clean UVs. Students learn what UVs are, when automatic unwrapping works, how to detect mistakes, and how to manually fix seams, islands, and material organization inside Blender. This module makes the model technically ready for baking and texture painting.

You walk away with:

A properly unwrapped model with usable UVs and organized materials.

Tools:

### Texture baking

Transfer high-poly detail onto the optimized low-poly mesh. Students learn the logic of baking, how normal maps and other texture maps are generated, and how to use both automated and manual baking workflows. The module covers practical baking pipelines inside Modddif, Blender, and Marmoset for production-ready results.

A baked low-poly model with essential texture maps ready for texturing.

### Painting + texturing

Build the final surface look of the character using AI-assisted and manual texturing workflows. Students create and refine base color, adjust materials, paint corrections by hand, and build core PBR maps including roughness, metallic, and emission. This is where the model gets its final visual identity and starts looking portfolio-ready.

You walk away with:

A fully textured character with production-ready PBR materials.

Prepare the character for movement by building or importing a skeleton and fixing deformation weights. Students learn the basics of rigging, compare different auto-rig solutions, and correct the most important weight-painting areas for clean animation. The module also includes a simple animation pass to test the character in motion.

A rigged and animation-ready character with clean deformations.

### Export to game engine

Finalize the character for real-time use and presentation. Students learn how to export properly, prepare textures and maps for engines, and bring the character into Unity and Unreal Engine 5. The final result is a game-ready asset that can be previewed, tested, and presented in a real engine environment.

You walk away with:

A game-ready character successfully imported into Unity and Unreal Engine 5.

### Do I need experience in 3D or programming?

No. The course is designed for beginners. Every step is explained from the very beginning. If you already have some experience, you’ll get through it faster and gain a deeper understanding.

### How long is the course?

The course is built around 5 days of structured content. How fast you move through it depends on how much time you can dedicate and how deeply you want to go. Most students finish in about a week, then keep returning to the modules as a reference while working on their own projects.

### What languages is the course available in?

Full voiceover in 5 languages (English, German, Spanish, Brazilian Portuguese, Russian) and subtitles in 9 languages (all voiceover languages plus Korean, Japanese, Chinese Simplified, French). Pick your language inside each lesson player, no extra setup.
