### Idea and Concepts

Turn a rough idea into a visual plan for the entire location, either from a hand-drawn sketch or a text prompt. Generate an isometric master reference showing every zone, then break it down into detailed references for individual assets. Learn the reference quality checklist and the batching trick to save tokens.

TOOLS:

You walk away with:

An isometric location reference + a full set of asset references ready for 3D generation.

### Generation of 3D Models

Turn your references into 3D assets using AI. Generate low-poly-first models in Tripo AI and find the right balance between detail and polygon count. Build simple modular elements by hand and paint them through AI when the generator can't deliver clean results. Learn when to generate with textures directly and when to go high-poly first and bake down.

TOOLS:

You walk away with:

A library of generated and hand-built 3D assets ready for optimization and texturing.

### Optimization and Refinement

Clean up your generated assets for production use. Fix geometry issues, reduce polygon count where needed, and prepare meshes for proper material assignment. Bake high-poly detail onto low-poly versions to keep visual quality without the performance cost. Cut the total material count from 60 to 80 down to roughly 20 for a clean, manageable project.

TOOLS:

You walk away with:

A clean, optimized asset library with correct geometry and reduced material count.

### Working with Materials

Make separately generated assets look like they belong in the same world. Correct clashing color palettes against your original references. Set up proper PBR materials: Roughness, Metallic, and Normal maps. Unify the visual style across every asset in your library. Learn how to fix color inconsistencies that AI generation always introduces.

TOOLS:

You walk away with:

A visually consistent asset library with unified PBR materials ready for scene assembly.

### Building Up the Scene [Exterior]

Start composing the outdoor environment. Work big to small: lay out mountain ranges and terrain first, then place large structures and foundations, then add modular buildings using symmetry workflows. Populate the scene with trees, flora using alpha masks, and detail props at points of interest. Check against your original reference constantly. If the scale is wrong, fix the big shapes first.

TOOLS:

You walk away with:

A fully assembled exterior environment with terrain, structures, vegetation, and detail elements in place.

### Building Up the Scene [Interior]

Build the interior spaces of your environment. Apply the same big-to-small logic inside closed spaces: walls and floors first, then furniture and structural elements, then small props and decorative details. Handle lighting setups specific to interiors and manage material behavior in enclosed spaces. Learn composition principles that make interiors feel lived-in and believable.

TOOLS:

You walk away with:

Completed interior spaces integrated into your environment that are furnished, detailed, and visually consistent with the exterior.

### Export and Scene Setup in Unreal Engine 5

Export the finished environment from Blender and bring it into Unreal Engine 5. Import meshes and textures, rebuild materials, organize the level, and configure lighting, cameras, and final presentation settings so the scene works correctly in real time. Learn the complete handoff from an assembled Blender scene to a polished environment ready to present in Unreal Engine 5.

TOOLS:

You walk away with:

A complete environment exported and set up in Unreal Engine 5 ready for final presentation.
